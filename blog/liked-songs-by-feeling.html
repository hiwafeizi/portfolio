I sorted 3,472 liked songs by how they feel

My Liked Songs is one long list: Persian rap next to lo-fi next to film scores. I wanted playlists by mood, not by genre and not by the day I liked something, so I built a small local app that listens to every song and sorts them for me.

TL;DR The app pulls my liked songs from Spotify, finds audio for each one, scores its mood with Essentia's pretrained models, clusters the scores into 24 moods, and pushes them back as playlists. 3,197 songs were analysed and 2,741 made it into a playlist. The map of all of them is below: click a playlist to see where it sits, press ▶ to listen.

The problem

Spotify used to hand out this data. Since late 2024, new apps get neither audio features (valence, energy, danceability) nor 30 second previews. So the app has to find the audio itself and judge the mood itself. And mood is harder than genre: a sad song and a happy song can share every instrument.

How it works

  • Find the audio. A 30 second preview from Deezer or iTunes, and for most songs the full track. More on both below; this was most of the work.
  • Score the mood. Essentia's models give every song 14 scores (happy, sad, relaxed, aggressive, party, danceable, dark, acoustic, electronic, instrumental, engaging, approachable, valence, arousal) plus an embedding of the sound itself. Essentia's model package has no Windows build, so the app runs its ONNX models with ONNX Runtime on a NumPy rebuild of its audio front end, which matches the original within 0.0003.
  • Find the moods. KMeans on the 14 scores, with valence and arousal counting double and a smaller dose of the sound embedding, so groups follow feeling first and sound second. I tried 25 and 40 groups, listened through them, and settled on 24, each named after its most typical songs.
  • Keep the playlists tight. A song far from its mood's centre is left out instead of forced in. At my setting that is 456 songs. The rest went to Spotify as 24 playlists, with songs that sit between two moods in both.

Getting the audio

Matching previews

The first version looked each song up on Deezer by its ISRC (the recording's ID) and fell back to Deezer's strict search, artist:"…" track:"…". That fallback returned nothing for every single query, and my title cleanup wiped Persian and Cyrillic titles to empty strings. So 29% of the library (1,020 songs) came back without audio. What fixed it:

  • Plain searches, two sources. Artist and title as free text on Deezer, then the iTunes Search API for what Deezer doesn't have. A hit only counts if title, artist and length all match; in a check of 79 hits, all 79 were the right song.
  • Matching that survives other scripts. Unicode normalization, Cyrillic to Latin, and a similarity threshold instead of exact equality, so a Cyrillic title can match its Latin spelling and a small spelling difference doesn't lose a song.
  • Downloads that don't give up. Deezer's preview server answers bursts with HTTP 429, which had saved 38 songs as empty files. Paced retries, and trying the next source when one fails, got almost all of them back.
  • A reason for every miss. Of the 304 songs still missing, 235 were taken down from Deezer, 45 were never there and 24 are blocked in my country. None of them is on iTunes either.

That took the songs without audio from 29% to under 9%.

30 seconds is not the song

A preview is usually the chorus, and Deezer and iTunes use the same 30 seconds (31 of 32 songs I compared). Clip quality was not the problem: re-encoding at Deezer's 128 kbps moved the mood scores by about 3% of the gap between two random songs. The excerpt was. In a 50 song test, analysing the whole song moved 4 in 10 songs to a different group.

So the app finds each song on YouTube Music by its title (including words like live, remix or slowed), artists, album and length, downloads it, and checks it is the same recording by finding the preview inside it. Then it analyses the full song and deletes the file. My first version took 7 seconds per song. One search request on YouTube Music's own catalogue, one download step and three songs at a time got that to about 1 second, a bit over an hour for the library. 2,929 songs were analysed whole, and 59 songs with no preview anywhere got added. 275 still have no audio at all.
